# LMS Light Strategy Shift — Summary for Staff

## The decision

We're moving away from a self-serve free trial model and adopting a **consultative two-track funnel** built around two new front doors: an AI-powered Plan Builder and a fully configured demo site. Self-serve subscription remains available for buyers who are ready, but the default path now runs through human contact before purchase.

This is a meaningful change in how we acquire and onboard customers, and it changes how visitors experience the website, how leads enter our pipeline, and how we close business.

---

## Why we're making this change

**Self-serve trials don't work for complex products like ours.** Historical data from John's previous Moodle hosting business showed roughly 1% trial-to-paid conversion. Trials work for products with instant value and low setup (Notion, Todoist) — they don't work for products that require content uploading, course structuring, and integration decisions before the value becomes obvious.

**Our actual buyers aren't always the operators.** Many visitors to our site are managers, founders, or department leads — people evaluating credibility on behalf of their organization, not necessarily the person who'll press the buttons. A blank trial site is the wrong artifact for that visitor. They need confidence and clarity, not a setup task.

**Human contact dramatically improves conversion.** In John's previous business, any point at which we established a real conversation with a prospect during their evaluation, conversion rates rose substantially. Our new funnel is built around making that contact the default rather than the exception.

**Our pricing positioning supports a consultative posture.** We sit between Google Drive and a $30,000 enterprise contract. Buyers in that gap aren't comparing checkout flows — they're comparing whether the platform actually fits their needs. A conversation answers that better than a trial does.

---

## What the new funnel looks like

The visitor journey now has four steps:

1. **Build your plan** — The visitor describes their training need to our AI Plan Builder, answers a few clarifying questions, and gets a custom implementation plan in about 10 minutes. The plan is downloadable and shareable, which matters for buyers who need to convince others internally.

2. **Explore a live demo site** — A fully configured LMS Light site with realistic courses, real reports, and one-click logins as admin, manager, or learner. This is the credibility check for visitors who want to see the product before committing to a conversation.

3. **Talk to our team** — A 30-minute discovery call where we walk through the visitor's plan, refine the implementation approach, and determine the right tier and any optional consulting support.

4. **Get the site live in days** — Once subscribed, we provision the site, help build out the first courses, and stay on standby through rollout.

Both the Plan Builder and the demo site are accessible directly from the homepage. Visitors choose which path matches where they are in their evaluation. Both lead to the same destination: a discovery call with our team.

---

## What's changing on the website

The website is being updated section by section to reflect the new strategy:

- **The hero** has been revised. New headline: *"The training platform between Google Drive and a $30,000 LMS contract."* The primary CTA is now *"Get your plan in 10 minutes →"* with secondary CTAs for booking a planning call and touring the demo site.
- **How We Work** has been rebuilt around the four-step funnel above.
- **Pricing tiers** will allow direct subscription via WooCommerce/Stripe — the *"Start free 7-days trial"* buttons are being replaced with tier-specific *"Choose Core / Grow / Pro"* CTAs.
- **The Enterprise callout** is being removed. It contradicts our positioning and dilutes the "no annual contract" promise.
- **The mid-page banner, footer, FAQ, and Features section** all still contain trial-era language and are being updated.

---

## What's changing about how we sell

**Trials are gone.** We don't offer them, and we don't promise them. Visitors who would have started a trial now build a plan, tour the demo, or book a call.

**Subscription remains self-serve.** Customers who've decided on a tier can subscribe directly via the pricing page. They land in their dashboard with full control over upgrades, downgrades, and cancellations. We don't gatekeep purchase.

**Consulting becomes a productized add-on.** Instead of bundling implementation help into the subscription cost (or providing it informally as a sales courtesy), we're packaging consulting as scoped, transparent add-ons attached to the subscription. A discovery call surfaces what level of support a customer needs; the customer purchases their tier plus the appropriate consulting package.

This separates "what the platform costs" from "what implementation help costs," which:

- Clarifies what customers are actually paying for
- Makes our team's billable time a paid line item rather than an unbilled cost of sale
- Improves unit economics on accounts that need significant hand-holding
- Lets self-sufficient customers buy just the platform without paying for help they don't need

The exact packaging structure (hour packs, named services, or both) is still being decided.

---

## What's not changing

- **Our pricing tiers** ($100/$200/$300 per month, no annual contract)
- **Our core product** (Moodle-powered LMS with pre-installed integrations and managed hosting)
- **Our positioning** (between scattered Drive folders and enterprise LMS contracts)
- **Our customers** — existing customers continue as they are; this is a change in how we acquire new ones

---

## What this means for our team

**For the team running customer-facing conversations:** discovery calls become the central event in our sales process. By the time a prospect is on a call, they've typically already built a plan and may have explored the demo site — they arrive with context. The call shifts from "let me show you the product" to "let's figure out exactly what you need." Tier recommendation and consulting scoping happen here.

**For the team building the demo site:** this is now a critical sales asset, not a marketing nice-to-have. The demo site is one of two front doors to our funnel and needs to hold up to scrutiny from both manager-level evaluators and operator-level buyers. Realistic seed data, polished aesthetics, and frictionless role-based access matter.

**For the team supporting the Plan Builder:** the tool is producing genuinely useful plans today, and conversations are being captured for review in the WordPress admin. Two refinements are on the roadmap: stronger email capture (so we can follow up with visitors who don't book immediately) and better integration with the demo site (so a generated plan naturally points toward the configured site as the next exploration step).

**For everyone:** the principle behind every piece of copy, every CTA, and every funnel decision is *"does this support the plan-and-demo strategy, or does it carry over from trial-first?"* If you see something on the site, in an email template, in a sales script, or in onboarding materials that contradicts the new direction, flag it. We're working through the website systematically but there will be artifacts of the old strategy in places we haven't gotten to yet.

---

## Why we're confident in this direction

Trials are abandoned 99% of the time for products like ours. Discovery calls — when they happen — close at dramatically higher rates. The Plan Builder and demo site are designed to multiply the number of qualified conversations our team has, by giving visitors low-commitment ways to engage that build toward a call rather than away from it.

If the new funnel underperforms, turning self-serve trials back on costs us nothing. The risk is asymmetric: we can always add a trial path back. Reversing a brand position from "self-serve SaaS" to "consultative platform" is much harder than the reverse.

This is a deliberate, reversible bet on a sales motion that better matches what our product actually is and how our buyers actually evaluate.
